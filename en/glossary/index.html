Glossary

Short explanations of terms that recur on the website. They are general explanations, not a diagnosis or a treatment guide.

Compulsion
A repeated action, outward or only in the head, that a person does so that fear or tension quickly eases. The relief is short and it strengthens the OCD loop.
Mental compulsion
A compulsion that takes place only in the head: repeating words or sentences, ruminating, going through memories, counting, an inner "cancelling" of a thought. It cannot be seen from outside.
Neutralisation
A hidden or mental step by which a person tries to "cancel" or "put right" an intrusive thought (for example saying a reassuring formula in their head). It works in a similar way to a compulsion.
Obsession
A recurring, unwanted and disruptive thought, image or urge that causes anxiety or tension. It tends to conflict with what a person wants and believes.
OCD
Obsessive-compulsive disorder: a condition in which obsessions and compulsions repeat, take up time and limit life. A diagnosis can be made only by a professional; this website does not make one.
Peer mentor
A person with their own experience of similar difficulties who accompanies and supports others alongside professional care. They do not diagnose, do not lead psychotherapy and do not prescribe medication.
Accommodation
When a loved one changes their behaviour because of OCD: answers reassurance questions, avoids triggers or takes part in rituals. They do it out of love and out of a wish to ease things, but they unintentionally keep the loop going.
Psychoeducation
A clear explanation of how certain difficulties work. It helps you understand what is going on, but it does not replace professional care.
Pure O
An informal term for OCD in which obsessions predominate and compulsions cannot be seen from outside because they take place mainly in the head.
Self-trust
Trust that when a difficulty comes I will look for a way to deal with it, and that I can ask for help. It is not a guarantee that I can handle anything. It is built through situations handled, not through affirmations.
Self-acceptance
I accept myself as a whole, including OCD, thoughts, anxiety and uncertainty. I do not have to like them, but I stop fighting them as an enemy.
The OCD loop
Tension leads to action (a compulsion), the action brings relief and the relief strengthens the loop. Working with OCD means staying in it differently: with the tension, without the action.
Trigger
An event, situation, feeling or thought that sets off an obsession and, with it, anxiety. It can also be an article, a news item or a bodily sensation.
Reassurance
Looking for confirmation that nothing will happen or that everything is all right: from loved ones, from a doctor, on the internet or from artificial intelligence. It gives short relief and keeps up the need to keep seeking reassurance.
Intrusive thoughts
Unwanted thoughts, images or urges that appear on their own and feel disruptive. Most people experience them. In OCD they "stick" because the person reacts to them with fear and rituals.
Avoidance
Not doing, or not going near, what triggers fear: places, people, objects, topics. It relieves in the short term, but in the long term it narrows life.

When to seek a professional

Contact a doctor or another professional if your worries and rituals take up a lot of time, limit your work, relationships or sleep, or if your condition is getting worse. Do not wait until it gets worse.

In an acute crisis or with thoughts of suicide or self-harm, call 112, 155 or the Czech first psychological help line 116 123. More on the page Help now.
