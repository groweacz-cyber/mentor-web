Emetophobic OCD

Emetophobic OCD is a form of OCD in which vomiting is the centre of the worry: the fear of it and rituals meant to prevent it.

Bc. Václav Kodeš

Peer mentor with personal experience of OCD

Last updated: 23 October 2026

What it is

A fear of vomiting can affect food, travel, social events and relationships. The person checks foods and use-by dates, avoids people who might be ill and keeps fixed rules around eating. Many of these steps are compulsions: they bring brief relief and in the end strengthen the fear.

Worries about nausea are common. Emetophobia (a specific phobia of vomiting) and OCD can overlap, and only a professional can tell them apart. If you have difficulties with eating, are losing weight or are troubled by physical symptoms, contact a doctor.

What obsessions and compulsions look like

Obsessions (examples)

  • What if I feel sick and cannot leave?
  • What if I catch something from someone who is ill?
  • What if the food harms me and I vomit?
  • Fear of the feeling of nausea and of not being able to cope with it.

Compulsions (examples)

  • Checking foods, use-by dates and how food is prepared.
  • Avoiding certain foods, restaurants, transport or people.
  • Rituals around food: fixed rules, portions, the time after a meal.
  • Monitoring bodily sensations for nausea.
  • Reassurance from others that they are well or that the food is fine.
  • Looking up information about illnesses and poisoning.

Common myths

Myth: It is just caution around food.
Reality: Reasonable caution protects. But if rules decide what you eat, where you go and whom you see, they limit life more than the threat they guard against.
Myth: If I avoid everything risky, I will be at peace.
Reality: Avoidance brings brief relief and narrows life. The fear usually grows and the number of "dangerous" situations increases.
Myth: It is just digestion.
Reality: A doctor assesses digestive problems. But a fear of vomiting can itself provoke physical sensations such as nausea, and the difficulties and the fear then strengthen each other.

How it is generally addressed by a professional

Professionals (a psychiatrist, clinical psychologist, psychotherapist) usually work with OCD using methods such as exposure and response prevention (ERP), or possibly inference-based cognitive behavioural therapy (I-CBT). A professional also determines the pace, the plan and possibly medication. This page is not a guide to carrying out such procedures on your own at home.

How to find professional help is summarised on the page Help now and in the section Where next: professional care.

With eating difficulties, weight loss or repeated physical symptoms it is good to have a general practitioner alongside a psychiatrist or psychologist.

Related types

  • Health OCD: Fear of illness, checking the body and seeking certainty that I am healthy.
  • Contamination: Fear of dirt, infection or chemicals and rituals around washing and cleaning.
  • Sensorimotor OCD: Constant awareness of breathing, blinking, swallowing or other bodily sensations.
  • Fear of going mad: Worry about losing control or sanity.

When to seek a professional

Contact a doctor or another professional if your worries and rituals take up a lot of time, limit your work, relationships or sleep, or if your condition is getting worse. Do not wait until it gets worse.

In an acute crisis or with thoughts of suicide or self-harm, call 112, 155 or the Czech first psychological help line 116 123. More on the page Help now.
